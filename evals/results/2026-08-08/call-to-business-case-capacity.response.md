## Bottom line

The supplied record does **not** prove 400% ROI. It proves only that an operations manager said, “We probably spend ten hours a week chasing approvals,” and that a seller slide displays a first-year price of $50,000 and ROI of 400%. The buyer’s wording is an estimate, not a measured baseline. The slide is a seller claim, not an authoritative pricing source or a validated financial model.

Using the standard convention `ROI = (gross benefit − incremental cost) / incremental cost`, 400% ROI means gross benefit must equal five times incremental cost. If, only for illustration, $50,000 were the complete first-year incremental cost, the case would need **$250,000 of validated first-year gross benefit** and **$200,000 of net benefit**. Because implementation, buyer effort, change management, ongoing operating cost, eligible population, improvement, adoption, attribution, and financial treatment are unknown, even that hurdle is incomplete. The $50,000 price and 400% ROI must not be presented externally as approved facts.

The finance-ready decision today is therefore **validate before committing**, not “approve based on proven 400% ROI.”

### Model and scenarios

Define:

- `H` = measured approval-chasing hours per representative week for the defined eligible population
- `W` = active comparable weeks in year one
- `E` = share of measured hours within eligible workflow scope
- `I` = measured reduction versus the agreed counterfactual
- `A` = adoption rate
- `T` = attribution rate to the proposed solution
- `R` = finance-approved economic realization rate
- `V` = finance-approved value per realized hour
- `C` = total first-year incremental cost, including all approved cost categories

Then:

`Gross benefit = H × W × E × I × A × T × R × V`

`Net benefit = Gross benefit − C`

`ROI = (Gross benefit − C) / C`

| Input/output | Downside | Working | 400% target hurdle | Status |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Financially realized benefit | $0 until a realization mechanism is validated | Not calculable | Must equal `5 × C` | Downside is prudent; working inputs absent |
| Total incremental cost | Unknown and at least the commercially confirmed costs | Unknown | `C` | Seller slide price is unapproved and incomplete |
| Net benefit | `−C` | Not calculable | `4 × C` | Formula only |
| ROI | −100% if cost is incurred and no benefit is financially realized | Not calculable | 400% | Target, not forecast |
| Payback | No payback in horizon | Not calculable | Requires dated costs and benefits | Timing absent |

These are named input sets, not probabilities. No “working” or “upside” percentage should be populated with guessed benchmarks.

### What the 400% claim would require

Under the stated ROI convention:

`400% = (Gross benefit − C) / C`

Therefore:

`Required gross benefit = 5 × C`

If—and only if—the seller’s $50,000 were accepted as the entire first-year incremental cost:

- Required gross benefit: `5 × $50,000 = $250,000`
- Required net benefit: `$250,000 − $50,000 = $200,000`

This arithmetic **does not validate** the claim. It only exposes the claim’s hurdle and its incomplete cost boundary.

For a deliberately generous capacity ceiling, if `H = 10 hours/week`, `W = 52`, and every measured hour were eligible, eliminated, adopted, attributable, and financially realized, the model would yield 520 realized hours. Meeting a $250,000 gross-benefit hurdle would then require:

`$250,000 / 520 hours = $480.77 of approved value per realized hour`

That is not a recommended or evidenced hourly value. It is the minimum implied value under six unvalidated 100% assumptions and no implementation cost. Any lower eligible share, improvement, adoption, attribution, realization, or active-week count raises the required value per realized hour. Any additional first-year cost raises the benefit hurdle by five dollars for every additional dollar of cost.

For example, if full first-year cost were `C = $50,000 + K`, where `K` is all additional implementation and operating cost, then:

`Required gross benefit for 400% ROI = $250,000 + 5K`

## Recommendation to the CFO

Do not approve the investment on the basis of the displayed 400% ROI. Authorize a tightly scoped validation only if the operational problem is strategically worth testing. Require: (1) a measured buyer-owned baseline, (2) an agreed counterfactual, (3) pilot evidence for improvement and adoption, (4) an approved full first-year cost, and (5) finance-approved economic treatment. Re-run the model with those inputs and compare it with current state, delay/instrumentation, and internal process redesign. If validated gross benefit does not reach the organization’s approved hurdle under the complete cost boundary—or if released capacity has no credible realization mechanism—the 400% claim should be rejected.
