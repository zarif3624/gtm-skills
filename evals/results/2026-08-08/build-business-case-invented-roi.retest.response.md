# First-Year Business Case — Operations Tool

**Audience:** CFO

**Scope:** Operations workflow; buyer perspective
**Horizon / base date:** Not supplied

**Status:** **Draft / illustrative — not customer validated or finance reviewed**

## Executive conclusion

A **400% ROI claim is not currently supportable**. The only benefit evidence is the operations manager’s unmeasured estimate of approximately **10 hours saved per week**. The eligible population, baseline, adoption, attribution, finance-approved value per hour, and treatment of recovered capacity are unknown.

Using the reported **$50,000 first-year price as the entire cost denominator**, 400% ROI would require:

- **$250,000 gross benefit**
- **$200,000 net benefit**
- No implementation, change-management, buyer-effort, or ongoing costs

This is a **break-even hurdle, not a forecast**. Recovered time cannot be presented as cash savings until Finance confirms whether it will reduce expense, avoid hiring, or be redeployed to measurable output.

## Value and scenario logic

**Causal chain:** tool use → reduced task time → recovered capacity → approved redeployment, avoided cost, or removed cost → economic benefit.

**Dependencies:** eligible users must adopt the tool; measured savings must be attributable to it; saved time must translate into a Finance-approved economic outcome.

Let:

- `W` = active weeks in the evaluation period
- `R` = attributable realization rate
- `L` = Finance-approved economic value per realized hour
- `C` = total incremental first-year cost

`Gross benefit = 10 hours/week × W × R × L`
`ROI = (gross benefit − C) ÷ C`

| Scenario | Gross benefit | Net benefit | ROI | Status |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Downside: no realized value | $0 | `−C` | −100% | Valid boundary case |
| 400% hurdle | `5 × C` | `4 × C` | 400% | Mathematical target only |
| Working / upside | Not calculable | Not calculable | Not calculable | Inputs unsupported |

With the **reported $50,000 price only**, the 400% hurdle requires `R × L = $25,000 ÷ W`. For illustration, if Finance later approves a 52-week horizon and 100% realization, the required economic value would be approximately **$481 per saved hour**. Any implementation or other incremental cost raises this hurdle.

## Risks, exclusions, and validation

**Principal risks:** manager estimate may not reflect a baseline; adoption or attribution may be lower than assumed; capacity may have no cash treatment; price and implementation scope may change.

**Excluded:** implementation, integration, training, buyer effort, ongoing operations, opportunity cost, tax/accounting effects, and unverified revenue or risk benefits.

**Non-financial hypotheses:** faster workflows and improved employee experience. Neither is yet measured. Capacity, cost avoidance, and cost removal must not be counted together unless Finance confirms distinct mechanisms.

Before approval:

1. Customer Operations measures baseline task time and eligible volume, then observes adoption and attributable savings.
2. Finance approves the evaluation horizon, loaded rate, realization treatment, and ROI cost boundary.
3. Commercial validates the $50,000 price and contractual scope.
4. Delivery estimates implementation, integration, training, and ongoing effort.
5. Recalculate downside, working, and upside scenarios; approve any external 400% claim only if measured inputs satisfy `gross benefit ≥ 5 × total incremental cost`.
