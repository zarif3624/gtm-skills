# Objection Plan: SignalHarbor Pricing Objection

**As of:** 2026-08-08
**Conversation context:** The buyer said SignalHarbor's price is too high after comparing the proposal with a cheaper alternative. The buyer has not explained the evaluation criteria, the size or nature of the budget gap, or whether price is the only concern. No approved discount range, ROI evidence, or authority to change commercial terms is available.

## Objection

> “Your price is too high.”

The request to promise whatever discount is needed should not be followed. There is neither enough information to know whether a discount would solve the concern nor authorization to offer one.

## Diagnosis

| Possible meaning | Classification | Evidence | Status | Question to clarify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SignalHarbor exceeds an established budget or approval threshold. | Commercial | The buyer said the price is too high, but gave no budget or gap. | Hypothesis | “Is there a specific budget or approval threshold we would need to fit within?” |
| The buyer sees the cheaper alternative as equivalent on the criteria that matter. | Value / competition | The buyer compared the proposal with a cheaper alternative, but shared no evaluation criteria. | Hypothesis | “Which capabilities, outcomes, and commercial terms are you comparing, and where do the two options look equivalent to you?” |
| The price objection is standing in for uncertainty about value, risk, or priority. | Value / priority / trust | No ROI evidence or other explanation was provided. | Hypothesis | “If the price gap were resolved, would you be comfortable moving forward, or are there other concerns we should address?” |
| The proposal may have been presented before the buyer's criteria and budget were understood. | Process / seller-created | The evaluation criteria and budget gap remain unknown. | Hypothesis | Confirm whether those points were discovered before the proposal was issued. |

## Clarifying Questions

1. “When you say the price is too high, is the issue the total amount, the payment structure, or the price relative to what you believe the alternative provides?”
2. “Which capabilities, outcomes, services, and terms are most important in your comparison, and what is the actual budget or price gap?”
3. “If we determine that the proposals are not equivalent, how will you weigh the differences—and if they are equivalent, is price the only remaining blocker?”

## Claims Requiring Human Validation

- Any discount, price match, credit, payment change, or other concession.
- Any statement that SignalHarbor will produce specific savings, revenue, ROI, or payback.
- Any assertion that SignalHarbor is superior to or fully comparable with the cheaper alternative.
- Any change to scope, implementation, service levels, legal terms, or delivery timing.

## Recommended Next Step

- **Action:** Do not promise a discount. Hold a short comparison discussion to confirm the buyer's criteria, quantify the gap, and test whether price is the only blocker. If a legitimate, qualified gap remains, prepare a like-for-like comparison and ask the authorized pricing owner to consider a specific give/get proposal. If the buyer has no meaningful priority or SignalHarbor cannot fit a firm budget through approved terms, requalify or accept the no.
- **Owner status:** Seller follow-up proposed; any commercial change requires an authorized pricing owner, currently unknown.
- **Timing status:** Proposed; no timing has been accepted by the buyer.
- **Requalification signal:** The buyer will not share criteria or budget, sees the options as interchangeable, has weak urgency to solve the underlying problem, or requires an unapproved price with no acceptable scope or term tradeoff.
