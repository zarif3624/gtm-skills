# Draft one-page business case: 400% ROI threshold

**Decision:** Whether to approve the proposed tool based on first-year economics
**Audience:** CFO
**Scope:** Unspecified operations population and workflow
**Horizon:** First year, subject to Finance confirmation
**Currency:** Reported as “$”; denomination unconfirmed
**Status:** **Illustrative—neither customer validated nor Finance reviewed**

## Executive summary

A 400% ROI claim is **not currently supportable**. The only value evidence is an operations manager’s unmeasured estimate of approximately 10 hours saved per week. This represents potential recovered capacity—not cash savings—and lacks a baseline, eligible population, adoption evidence, attribution method, realization plan, and approved labor rate.

Using the reported $50,000 price as the entire first-year cost, 400% ROI would require **$250,000 of Finance-recognized gross benefit**. Even under a ceiling case of 52 active weeks, full adoption, and full attribution, that requires an approved value of **$480.77 per recovered hour**. Any implementation cost, buyer effort, downtime, lower adoption, or partial attribution raises the threshold.

**Recommendation:** Do not approve the purchase on a 400% ROI claim. First validate the hours, economic treatment, all-in cost, and realization mechanism.

## Alternatives and evidence

| Alternative | Operational/economic effect | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continue current state | No new cost; current workload and economic impact are unknown | Unknown |
| Defer and validate | Preserves optionality while establishing a measured baseline and cost boundary | Proposed |
| Purchase tool | Manager reports ~10 hours/week of potential capacity; monetary benefit unproven | Reported |
| First-year price | $50,000; source, scope, treatment, and approval not supplied | Reported, unverified |

## Value and cost model

**Causal chain:** tool adoption → fewer hours required → recovered capacity → approved redeployment, avoided cost, or removed cost → Finance-recognized benefit.

The chain is unvalidated at every step after the manager’s estimate. Time saved must not be treated as cash savings without an authorized realization plan.

**All-in first-year cost**

`C = subscription + implementation + buyer effort + change management + ongoing operation`

Only a reported subscription figure of `$50,000` is available. All other cost components are unknown and excluded from the threshold illustration.

## Scenario and break-even analysis

Let:

- `H = 10 reported potential hours/week`
- `W = active weeks`
- `A = realized adoption`
- `T = attributable share`
- `R = Finance-approved value per recovered hour`
- `C = all-in incremental cost`

`Gross benefit = H × W × A × T × R`
`ROI = (gross benefit − C) ÷ C`

| Case | Result |
| --- | --- |
| No Finance-recognized treatment of capacity | Gross benefit not established; ROI cannot support approval |
| Reported capacity only | 10 hours/week; dollar benefit and ROI remain unknown |
| 400% ROI threshold | Gross benefit must equal `5 × C` |
| Reported price only | At `C = $50,000`, required gross benefit is `$250,000` |
| Maximum calendar ceiling | At `W=52`, `A=100%`, and `T=100%`, required `R = $480.77/hour` |

General break-even formula for the requested claim:

`Required R = 5C ÷ (10 × W × A × T)`

Lower adoption, partial attribution, fewer active weeks, or additional costs increase the required hourly value.

## Risks, exclusions, and validation required

Key disconfirming evidence is the absence of a measured baseline, observed adoption, eligible population, attribution method, approved loaded rate, and confirmed capacity-realization action. Pricing provenance, implementation cost, customer Finance treatment, time horizon, and approval are also unknown. No revenue, risk reduction, or other benefits are quantified; this avoids double counting.

Before approval:

1. Measure current hours and post-tool hours for a defined workflow and population.
2. Confirm adoption, active weeks, and an attribution method.
3. Have Finance approve the treatment of recovered capacity and the applicable value rate.
4. Obtain approved pricing and quantify implementation, buyer, change, and operating costs.
5. Recalculate ROI using the validated inputs and document Finance review.
