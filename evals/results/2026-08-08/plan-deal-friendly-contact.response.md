## Deal thesis

Juniper Grid is not yet a forecastable close. The main contact is accessible and responsive, but there is no evidence that they have shared decision information, introduced the buying group, sold internally, or taken an internal action; they should be treated as a friendly contact and potential champion, not a verified champion. The close strategy is to help this contact test and build an internal case, earn stakeholder access through them, and expose procurement and security requirements early. The fastest credible path is through procurement—not around it—by identifying its intake, evidence, owners, sequencing, and lead times before a commercial commitment is assumed.

## Decision and commercial path

- **Decision criteria:** Unknown. Validate business outcomes, technical requirements, risk requirements, time-to-value, and commercial criteria with the appropriate owners.
- **Decision process:** Unknown. Ask what decision is being made, who recommends, who approves, what meeting or event produces the decision, and what must be true beforehand.
- **Procurement, security, and legal:** Unknown and therefore a critical-path risk. Do not bypass these functions or seek a side agreement. Determine whether reviews can run in parallel, whether a standard security package can satisfy initial diligence, which contract form is preferred, what thresholds trigger extra approvals, and whether vendor onboarding must precede signature.
- **Funding:** Unknown. Confirm budget source, amount or approval threshold, budget owner, and timing.
- **Implementation:** Unknown. Confirm the implementation owner, resources, data or integration dependencies, deployment sequence, and measurable first-value milestone.
- **Close-date rule:** Establish a buyer-accepted date only after working backward from confirmed decision, security, procurement, legal, and signature milestones. Until then, keep the opportunity out of commit.
