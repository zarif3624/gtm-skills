# Initial ICP and Logistics Segment Score — HarborMetric

**As of:** 2026-08-08
**Decision this ICP supports:** Whether HarborMetric should concentrate its next founder-led validation effort on regional logistics firms.
**Evidence window and sample:** Three founder-referred paying customers: two regional logistics firms and one retailer. One logistics customer renewed; the other logistics customer and the retailer are too new for retention assessment. There is no structured loss, retention, usage, outcome, pipeline, or segment market data. Because every customer came through founder referrals, the sample is small and selection-biased.

## ICP Statement

HarborMetric’s candidate ICP is a regional logistics firm with a verified, costly problem that HarborMetric directly solves, a named operational owner accountable for the outcome, the data and process readiness needed to adopt the product, and an active reason to act. Regional logistics is the leading segment hypothesis—not yet a validated ICP—because it accounts for two of three purchases and the only observable renewal. The hypothesis fails if independent logistics prospects do not share a repeatable job, cannot reach measurable value, or do not buy and retain without founder-relationship effects.

**Decision:** The logistics segment cannot yet receive a defensible point estimate or standard tier because its evidence range crosses every threshold. Treat it as a **Tier 3 validation priority**, not a scaled go-to-market commitment. The evidence floor is only 20/100; the ceiling reflects unknowns, not demonstrated fit. The renewal is a positive signal, but one founder-referred renewal cannot establish retention or segment superiority, and the retailer is not yet a negative counterexample.

## Counterexamples and Unknowns

**Known counterexamples:** None. The retailer purchase shows that logistics is not the only segment able to buy, but the retailer is too new to compare on value or retention. There are no recorded losses or non-renewals.

**Most consequential unknowns, in collection order:**

1. What exact job, painful current state, and measurable outcome caused each customer to buy?
2. Why did the logistics customer renew: realized product value, expected future value, switching cost, or founder relationship?
3. Do the two logistics customers share meaningful attributes beyond the broad industry label—size, operating model, geography, stack, process maturity, constraints, and buying group?
4. Did users activate, adopt repeatedly, and improve the target outcome? How long did value take?
5. What triggers, objections, sales effort, price tolerance, delivery cost, and support burden characterize the segment?
6. How do independently sourced logistics prospects, losses, and no-decisions compare with retailer and other segments?
7. Is the reachable regional-logistics segment large enough for HarborMetric’s goals? Assess only after the repeatable job and firmographic boundary are clear.

Keep the retailer as a live comparison until it has enough outcome and retention evidence to evaluate fairly.
