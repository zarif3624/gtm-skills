# Ideal Customer Profile: HarborMetric

**As of:** 2026-08-08
**Decision this ICP supports:** Whether regional logistics should receive HarborMetric’s next focused validation and prospecting effort.
**Evidence window and sample:** Three founder-referred customers: two regional logistics firms and one retailer. One logistics customer renewed; the other logistics customer and the retailer are too new for retention assessment. There is no structured loss, retention, usage, segment-market, or delivery-economics data.

## ICP Statement

**Working ICP hypothesis:** HarborMetric’s initial ICP is a regional logistics firm with a documented, material business problem that HarborMetric is designed to solve, a named owner of that problem, the operational capacity to implement the product and realize value, and a current event making action timely. This is a segment hypothesis, not a confirmed ICP: logistics represents two of three founder-referred purchases and the only observable renewal, but referral bias, the tiny sample, missing product-usage evidence, and the absence of comparable outcomes prevent a causal conclusion that logistics predicts success.

## Counterexamples and Unknowns

**Known counterexamples and limits**

- The retailer purchase challenges any claim that logistics is the only viable segment, although the retailer is too new to compare on retention.
- Only one logistics customer has reached an observable renewal, so that renewal cannot establish a segment pattern.
- The second logistics customer is too new to confirm or contradict the hypothesis.
- Every purchase came through a founder referral, so acquisition repeatability is completely untested.
- There are no recorded losses or no-decisions against which to test the proposed criteria.

**Highest-value unknowns, in order**

1. The precise job-to-be-done, painful current state, intended use case, and measurable value outcome for each customer.
2. Whether the renewed logistics customer renewed because of realized product value, relationship effects, switching cost, contract structure, or another cause.
3. Comparable activation, usage, time-to-value, support effort, delivery cost, retention, and expansion outcomes for all three customers.
4. Which company attributes—size, geography, operating model, process maturity, stack, or constraints—actually separate successful from unsuccessful accounts.
5. The buying roles, trigger events, decision process, sales effort, pricing, and economics of each deal.
6. Loss and no-decision reasons from non-customer opportunities and segment-level demand or market evidence.
