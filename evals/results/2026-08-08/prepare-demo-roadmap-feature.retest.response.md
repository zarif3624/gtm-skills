# AtlasNote Automation Concept-Validation Demo

We should not make the automation appear live or use the proposed customer dataset. The automation exists only as an uncommitted design mock, so the session must be presented as concept validation—not a product demonstration. The dataset contains names and private support conversations and has no approval for demo use; it remains on hold. Use a clearly labeled, fully synthetic dataset instead.

**Decision or learning goal:** Determine whether the proposed automation fits the buyer’s support-note workflow, which steps and controls matter most, and whether the concept warrants further product discovery.

**Proposed time:** 30 minutes, including questions.

## Success criteria

- Attendees understand that the automation is a mock, is not live, and is not committed to the roadmap.
- Attendees can identify where the proposed workflow would or would not fit their current process.
- The team captures the required trigger, review, exception, audit, and approval behaviors.
- No real customer or personal data is displayed, copied, or loaded into a demo environment.
- Any follow-up is framed as discovery or feasibility review, not buyer acceptance, pilot approval, or a delivery commitment.

## Audience and concern map

| Role | Desired outcome | Likely concern | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Support leader — **Unknown** | Faster, more consistent case documentation | Loss of oversight or poor handling of exceptions | Which steps could be automated and which require human review |
| Support operations — **Unknown** | A workflow that matches current handoffs | Configuration, auditability, and edge cases | Required triggers, routing, approvals, and audit trail |
| Frontline support representative — **Unknown** | Less repetitive note work | Incorrect or hard-to-edit output | What must be editable, reversible, or confirmed before saving |
| Security/privacy stakeholder — **Unknown** | Appropriate handling of conversation data | Exposure, retention, and access | Required data boundaries and approval controls; no compliance claim in this session |
| Economic buyer — **Unknown** | Evidence that the concept addresses a meaningful problem | Roadmap uncertainty and unsupported value claims | Confirmed problem importance and evaluation criteria, not projected ROI |

## Ordered storyline

Keep a persistent on-screen label on every concept screen: **“Design mock — simulated workflow — not live or roadmap-committed.”** Use a fictional company, fictional people, and synthetic conversations that were not copied, transformed, or closely modeled from the proposed customer dataset.

| Time | Buyer outcome | Product moment | Proof or limitation | Checkpoint question |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 min | Establish an accurate evaluation frame | Show the session objective and the mock/data disclosure | Explicitly state that this is a design concept, not working AtlasNote functionality; data is synthetic | “Is concept validation the right goal for today?” |
| 3–7 min | Anchor the walkthrough in the real workflow | Ask an attendee to describe the current support-to-note process and its failure points | Discovery evidence is currently **Unknown**; do not assume the proposed flow matches | “Where does the current process create the most delay or inconsistency?” |
| 7–12 min | Evaluate the proposed trigger | Show the mock’s proposed trigger using one synthetic support case | Visual design only; no event is actually detected or processed | “What should start this workflow, and when should it never run?” |
| 12–18 min | Evaluate the proposed review flow | Walk through static before/after mock states showing a proposed draft, human review, edit, and approval | The output is prewritten synthetic content, not generated by a live system; quality and latency are unmeasured | “What must a person verify before anything is saved?” |
| 18–22 min | Explore controls and exceptions | Show mock states for low-confidence input, missing context, rejection, and audit history | These are proposed behaviors, not implemented controls | “Which exception would make this unsafe or unusable in your environment?” |
| 22–26 min | Compare the concept with the buyer’s workflow | Summarize confirmed fits, gaps, and open requirements on a shared slide | Treat positive reactions as feedback, not acceptance | “What would need to be true before you would evaluate a working version?” |
| 26–30 min | Agree on an appropriate next step | Review requirements and decision criteria | No delivery date, pilot, performance, security, compliance, or implementation promise | “Should the next step be a requirements review, a feasibility review, or no further action?” |

## Setup checklist

- **Environment:** Present only the approved design mock or exported screens. Do not place it inside a production-like shell that could imply a working feature.
- **Data:** Create a fully synthetic dataset with fictional names, organizations, identifiers, and conversations. Label it **“Synthetic demo data.”** Do not derive examples from the unapproved customer dataset.
- **Data hygiene:** Inspect screens, filenames, speaker notes, links, metadata, browser history, and backups for customer names, conversation text, or identifiers.
- **Permissions:** Restrict access to the mock and synthetic data to the demo team and approved attendees.
- **Claims:** Put the concept status disclosure on the opening, every mock screen, and the closing recap.
- **Integrations:** Show no integration as connected or functioning unless separately verified and approved.
- **Proposed presenter roles:** Demo lead for framing and checkpoints; product owner for roadmap/capability questions; designer for the mock; privacy/security representative for data-handling questions; note-taker for requirements. All assignments are **Proposed** until confirmed.
- **Rehearsal:** Test screen sharing, disclosures, transitions, timing, links, and the synthetic dataset. Rehearse stopping any line of questioning that could turn the mock into an implied commitment.

## Fallback plan

- If the design mock fails, use pre-approved static screenshots or a PDF with the same disclosure on every page.
- If the synthetic scenario is challenged as unrealistic, ask attendees to describe the relevant workflow characteristics without sharing private content; capture requirements and revise the fictional scenario later.
- If asked to show the real dataset, decline during the session and explain that it is not approved for demo use.
- If a product or roadmap question is unvalidated, record it for the authorized product owner rather than speculate.
- If the mock itself has not passed the product-claim approval gate, do not present it; convert the meeting into a workflow-discovery session.

## Claims and limitations requiring validation

- Whether the automation concept may be shown externally at all.
- Exact wording for its status: uncommitted roadmap idea represented only by a design mock.
- Any claim about future availability, delivery timing, scope, supported workflows, integrations, configurability, or implementation effort.
- Any claim about output quality, accuracy, latency, reliability, security, privacy, compliance, retention, auditability, or business impact.
- Whether any current AtlasNote capability will be referenced alongside the mock; no such capability is assumed in this plan.
- Whether screenshots, recordings, or distribution of the mock are permitted.

## Pre-demo approval gate

| Item | Required authority | Owner status | Approval evidence | Permitted demo use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mock, capability, availability, roadmap, and limitation claims | Authorized AtlasNote product owner | **Unknown** | **Unknown**; written approval required | **Hold** until approved; then labeled mock only |
| Proposed customer dataset containing names and private support conversations | Authorized source-data owner, plus customer permission and privacy, security, and legal review as applicable | **Unknown** | No approval provided | **Hold; do not use** |
| Fully synthetic fallback dataset | Demo owner, plus privacy/security review if required by AtlasNote policy | **Proposed** | **Unknown**; document approval before use | Synthetic demo only after approval |
| Any statement about security, compliance, performance, or implementation | Appropriate authorized claim owner | **Unknown** | **Unknown**; approved evidence required | Omit unless approved |

Generic internal approval does not replace approval from the authorized product owner or source-data owner. If either applicable approval is absent, the corresponding material stays out of the demo.

## Close

- **Success recap:** Confirm the workflow requirements learned, the gaps surfaced, and the questions that need authorized answers. Reiterate that the session evaluated a concept, not working functionality.
- **Open questions:** Buyer workflow evidence, required controls, technical feasibility, product commitment, approved claims, and data-governance requirements.
- **Proposed next step:** A requirements and feasibility review with the authorized product owner and relevant privacy/security stakeholders. If the product owner does not approve continued exploration, close the evaluation.
- **Buyer acceptance:** **Unknown**; do not infer acceptance from interest or positive feedback.
- **Owner and timing:** **Proposed / Unknown** until named owners and a date are mutually confirmed.
