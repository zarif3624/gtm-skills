## Executive Customer Summary

Pine Health purchased standard onboarding. The buyer also expects a custom integration and a launch within 30 days, but neither expectation is confirmed by the signed order or accepted by the implementation team. A sales call note records that the integration was described to the buyer as likely easy; it does not establish scope, feasibility, approval, ownership, or timing.

The priority before kickoff is to preserve the buyer's goals without turning either expectation into an implementation commitment. Sales and the receiving implementation lead must jointly resolve or establish a written, customer-safe validation plan for the integration and launch target. Until that happens, the permitted position is that both are important inputs under review and remain unconfirmed.

## Implementation Prerequisites and Risks

- **Prerequisites:** Named customer and implementation owners; agreed onboarding plan; customer availability; access and readiness requirements; definition of launch; acceptance criteria. All are currently unknown.
- **Integrations and data:** The requested systems, use cases, data flows, authentication, environments, dependencies, testing approach, and support model are not documented.
- **Security or legal follow-up:** Unknown. Determine whether integration work triggers security, privacy, data-processing, or legal review before scope or timing is approved.
- **Training and change management:** Requirements, audiences, and owners are unknown and should be established during onboarding planning.
- **Primary risks:** Expectation mismatch at kickoff; unapproved custom work; feasibility and resourcing uncertainty; undefined customer dependencies; and a 30-day target being mistaken for a committed launch date.
- **Mitigation:** Enter kickoff with one aligned statement from Sales and implementation, record decisions in writing, and use milestone-relative planning until owners accept dates.

You will not need to repeat the context already captured. During planning, we will validate the integration's technical requirements, feasibility, ownership, and scope, then confirm how it fits into the delivery plan. In parallel, we will define what launch includes and review dependencies and resourcing so we can confirm the earliest achievable date.

At this stage, the custom integration and the 30-day launch date are not yet confirmed commitments. We will document decisions, owners, and timing with you as the review is completed. This gives both teams one clear plan while keeping Pine Health's priorities central to kickoff.
