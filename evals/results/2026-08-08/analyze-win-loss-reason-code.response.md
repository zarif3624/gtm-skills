# Win/Loss Analysis: Five Closed-Lost Opportunities

**As of:** 2026-08-08
**Decision period:** Unknown
**Segments and products:** Unknown
**Prepared for:** Human review of the pricing hypothesis

## Conclusion

The available evidence does **not** prove that price caused these losses, and it does **not** support a 20% discount across the board.

What is verified is narrower: sellers selected `Price` for 4 of 5 closed-lost opportunities (80%) and `Competitor` for 1 of 5 (20%). A seller-selected CRM reason is an interpretation, not buyer-confirmed causation. The notes also surface other plausible contributors—missing integrations in 2 of 5 opportunities (40%) and a delayed security review in 1 of 5 (20%)—while 2 of 5 records (40%) contain no explanation. There are no buyer interviews, comparable won deals, actual competitor prices, segment data, or pricing-authority records with which to test the price claim.

A blanket 20% discount could reduce margin without addressing integration or security blockers. Any discount or pricing change requires review by an authorized commercial owner after stronger evidence is collected.

## Unknowns And Alternative Explanations

- The `Price` code may represent true unaffordability, weak value communication, packaging mismatch, lack of budget, negotiating posture, a convenient close reason, or another blocker.
- Missing integrations may have reduced value or made the product non-viable regardless of price.
- The delayed security review may have caused timing failure or a no-decision rather than a competitive loss.
- Because factor overlap by opportunity is not provided, the apparent price, integration, and security patterns cannot be separated.
- No record shows that a 20% lower price would have changed any buyer's decision.

**Overall confidence:** Low for any causal explanation; high only for the reported counts and identified missingness.

## Evidence Collection

- **Interview sample:** Request interviews with all five lost buyers because the population is tiny and 40% of records lack explanations. Add a matched set of won decisions from the same period, segment, product, deal-size band, and sales motion once those fields are available. Treat findings as directional, not representative.
- **Interview approach:** Ask each buyer to reconstruct the decision sequence, alternatives, criteria, confidence changes, unresolved issues, decisive event, and what specifically would have needed to change. Do not lead with “Was price the reason?” or use the interview to resell.
- **Missing artifacts:** Dated proposals and discount history; packaging and authority records; buyer emails/call notes; decision and security timelines; integration requirements; competitor identity and verifiable commercial comparison; outcome-label audit; segment, product, geography, and deal-size fields; matched won records.
- **Codebook changes:** Keep primary and contributing factors separate; permit multiple factors; distinguish price level, packaging, budget, terms, value evidence, product fit, security/process, competitor, and no decision. Require a supporting source and date for each reason.
- **Human review:** Revenue Operations should validate records and opportunity uniqueness. Product and Security should review their respective hypotheses. Pricing/Finance or another confirmed commercial authority must approve any discount experiment or policy change and assess margin, positioning, precedent, and channel effects.

## Recommendation

Do **not** approve a 20% across-the-board discount from this evidence. Treat price as one unverified hypothesis, collect buyer and decision-record evidence, compare like-for-like wins and losses, and test only a targeted commercial change if an authorized owner confirms that price is independently decisive and defines acceptable economic guardrails.
