# Alpine Freight Discovery Plan

The aim is not to get the prospect to “admit” a predetermined multimillion-dollar cost. No available source supports that claim. The aim is to help Alpine Freight build a defensible, buyer-owned estimate from its own examples and data—and to accept a smaller impact, no priority, or no fit if that is what discovery shows.

## Call objective

Determine whether the current operations process creates a material, measurable problem worth investigating further, what evidence would establish its size, and who should participate in that evaluation.

**Decision this call should enable:** Whether there is enough verified impact and priority to justify a scoped follow-up analysis.

## Proposed agenda

1. Confirm the purpose and what would make the conversation useful.
2. Understand the current workflow through one recent example.
3. Trace frequency, downstream effects, and measurable costs.
4. Discuss the desired outcome and how Alpine Freight would evaluate change.
5. Agree whether to continue, pause, or bring in other stakeholders.

**Permission-based opening:**

“We saw that Alpine Freight is hiring around operations modernization. That tells us change may be underway, but it does not tell us which processes matter most, whether this issue is costly, or whether it is a priority. Would it be useful to spend the first part of the call on one recent workflow example, then see whether there is enough measurable impact to warrant a next step? If there is not, we can stop there.”

## Facts, hypotheses, and unknowns

| Item | Status | Evidence | How to validate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alpine Freight appears to be modernizing operations. | Inferred | Public job post. | Ask what “modernization” means in practice and which workflows it affects. |
| Some current workflows may involve avoidable manual work, delay, or exceptions. | Hypothesis | Modernization activity can indicate process change, but the post does not identify a problem. | Ask for a recent workflow example and where work slows, repeats, or escalates. |
| Repeated operational friction could create material aggregate impact at sufficient volume. | Hypothesis | This is a general impact mechanism, not an Alpine Freight fact. | Quantify buyer-provided frequency, volume, time, and consequence. |
| The operations manager and analyst may hold complementary workflow and data context. | Hypothesis | Attendee titles only. | Ask what each owns, measures, influences, and does not know. |
| Cost, delay, urgency, decision criteria, budget, and decision authority. | Unknown | No source quantifies or establishes them. | Discover without suggesting an answer. |

## Attendee map

| Attendee | Likely perspective to explore—not assume | What to learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operations manager | Day-to-day workflow, exceptions, staffing, service consequences | Process ownership, severity, affected teams, priority, and who approves change |
| Analyst | Definitions, baselines, reporting, available data, confidence in estimates | Which measures exist, how reliable they are, and who can validate financial impact |

Their decision roles are **Unknown**. Do not treat either attendee as a champion, economic buyer, approver, or data owner until they say so.

## Priority questions

Use these as a conversation guide, not a questionnaire.

| Question | Why it matters | Best follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Before we dive in, what would make this conversation useful for each of you?” | Surfaces priorities and differences between attendees. | “Is there anything you do not want us to spend time on?” |
| “When Alpine Freight talks about modernizing operations, what is actually changing—and what is driving that work?” | Validates the only public signal without treating it as urgency or pain. | “Which parts are committed, exploratory, or out of scope?” |
| “Walk me through the last time this process created extra work, a delay, or an exception—from the first trigger to resolution.” | Grounds discovery in observed behavior rather than agreement with a claim. | “Who touched it, how long did each step take, and where did it wait?” |
| “How typical was that example, and how often does something similar happen?” | Establishes whether the example can be generalized. | “What period or data source would let us verify that?” |
| “What did that incident affect downstream?” | Finds consequences in the buyer’s language. | “Did it affect labor, capacity, service, expediting, penalties, claims, write-offs, revenue, or something else—and which of those can you measure?” |
| “Which numbers do you already trust, and which would need analysis?” | Separates evidence from estimates. | “Who owns each number, and what range would they consider credible?” |
| “Would it be useful to estimate the annual impact together using only figures you are comfortable supplying?” | Requests permission before quantification. | “What volume, frequency, time, and unit-cost assumptions should we use?” |
| “If nothing changed over the next quarter, what would happen?” | Tests consequence and priority without asserting urgency. | “Would that be acceptable, and why?” |
| “How would your team judge whether a change worked?” | Surfaces success measures and decision criteria. | “What baseline and review period would make that judgment credible?” |
| “If the impact is worth pursuing, who else would need to validate the problem, the economics, and the decision?” | Maps the decision process without assuming attendee authority. | “What would each person need to see?” |

## Quantification path

Build the estimate live only with the participants’ permission. Keep each input labeled as **Verified**, **Buyer estimate**, or **Unknown**.

Possible annualized impact components:

- Incident frequency × average staff time × buyer-provided loaded labor cost.
- Affected volume × incremental handling or rework cost.
- Buyer-verified expediting, overtime, penalties, claims, write-offs, or lost contribution.
- Capacity displaced by the process, counted only if Alpine Freight can explain how that capacity translates into economic value.

Avoid double-counting overlapping effects. Show a low, expected, and high range when inputs are uncertain. If the supported result is not in the millions, say so plainly; the purpose is a credible estimate, not a target number.

## Likely branches and follow-ups

- **They describe a painful example but lack data:** Ask which system, report, or person could validate frequency and consequence. Propose a short data review rather than estimating from memory.
- **They say the impact is “huge” or “millions”:** Ask, “What inputs make up that estimate, and which are measured versus assumed?” Do not repeat the figure as fact until it is supported.
- **They see inefficiency but no financial consequence:** Explore service, capacity, risk, and employee effects. If none matters enough to act on, pause.
- **The example is rare or atypical:** Ask for a more representative case. Do not annualize an outlier.
- **The attendees disagree:** Capture both views, identify the disputed input, and ask what evidence would resolve it.
- **Modernization is unrelated to this workflow:** Acknowledge the mismatch and determine whether there is another relevant process; otherwise disqualify respectfully.
- **They cannot name a consequence of doing nothing:** Treat priority as unproven and avoid manufacturing urgency.

## Reserve questions

- “Who experiences this process differently from your team?”
- “What workarounds have people created, and what do those workarounds cost or risk?”
- “What have you already tried, and what prevented it from working?”
- “Is the issue concentrated by location, customer segment, lane, shipment type, or time period?”
- “What would make this initiative lose priority?”
- “How are operational investments normally evaluated and approved here?”

## What not to ask

- Do not ask, “This must be costing you millions, right?” or any variation that embeds the desired answer.
- Do not ask for budget before establishing a problem, measurable impact, and decision path.
- Do not ask generic questions already answered by the public job post.
- Do not ask stacked questions that prevent a concrete answer.
- Do not press either attendee to speak for finance, executives, or other teams whose views they do not own.

## Proof or demo material to hold in reserve

- A blank impact worksheet that exposes every input and assumption.
- A simple current-state workflow map to complete using the buyer’s example.
- Relevant proof from a genuinely comparable customer only after the problem is clear, with scope and claims sourced.
- A narrowly tailored demo only after Alpine Freight identifies the workflow and success measure.

Do not lead with product slides or use a benchmark as if it proves Alpine Freight’s cost.

## Signals

**Continue if:** The participants identify a recurring problem, a meaningful consequence, a path to validate the data, and a reason to consider change.

**Pause or disqualify if:** The issue is rare or low-impact; modernization is unrelated; there is no consequence to inaction; reliable inputs cannot be obtained; the relevant owners will not engage; or the status quo is acceptable.

**Do not assume:** Multimillion-dollar impact, urgency, executive sponsorship, budget, authority, access to data, or agreement between attendees.

## Closing recap and next-step standard

**Recap template:**

“What we heard is that [workflow] creates [verified effect] about [verified or buyer-estimated frequency]. The inputs we trust are [inputs], while [gaps] remain unverified. You described the consequence as [buyer’s words] and the priority as [buyer’s stated priority]. Have we captured that accurately?”

**Proposed next step:** If the participants confirm material impact and want to continue, hold a scoped validation session using Alpine Freight’s data, with the people who own the workflow, metrics, and financial assumptions.

- Buyer acceptance: **Unknown until explicitly accepted**
- Owner: **Proposed—operations manager to identify the workflow owner; analyst to identify available data; both subject to confirmation**
- Timing: **Proposed—after the relevant data and stakeholders are available; no date assumed**
- Success standard: A mutually accepted baseline, transparent impact range, named decision participants, and a clear go/pause decision

If those conditions are not met, the useful close is to document the gaps and pause rather than force a next meeting.
