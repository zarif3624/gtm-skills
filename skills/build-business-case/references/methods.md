# Business-Case Methods

## Decision Frame

Name the decision before choosing a metric. Record the audience, entity or workflow in scope, perspective, base date, time horizon, currency, and alternatives. A buyer choosing whether to fund a pilot needs a different model from finance deciding on a multi-year rollout.

## Evidence Ledger

For every material input, preserve:

- definition and unit;
- value or range;
- evidence status;
- source and as-of date;
- population and time coverage;
- validation owner and current approval status.

Supplier benchmarks and seller hypotheses may support a test design, but they are not customer baselines.

## Value Chain

Trace each benefit through four layers:

1. product or process activity;
2. operational change;
3. business outcome;
4. economic treatment.

Document what else could cause the change and what must happen for value to be realized. Remove overlapping benefits or show them as alternative treatments.

## Core Calculations

Use only when every input has a defined unit and compatible time period:

- `gross benefit = eligible volume × measured improvement per unit × approved value per unit`
- `net benefit = gross benefit - incremental cost`
- `ROI = (gross benefit - incremental cost) / incremental cost`
- `break-even improvement = incremental cost / (eligible volume × approved value per unit)`

State whether the ROI denominator includes subscription only, first-year cash cost, total implementation cost, or another approved boundary. Payback requires the timing of costs and realized benefits, not just annual totals. Present value requires an approved discount rate and dated cash flows; leave it unknown otherwise.

## Economic Treatment

- **Recovered capacity:** hours potentially available for other work. Do not call it cash savings without a confirmed action and financial treatment.
- **Cost avoided:** a future cost that would otherwise occur. Verify the counterfactual, timing, and owner.
- **Cost removed:** an expense actually eliminated. Confirm the mechanism and date.
- **Revenue influenced:** revenue associated with the change. Do not treat it as incremental or attributable without a defensible counterfactual; do not treat revenue as profit.
- **Risk reduction:** describe exposure and control change. Do not monetize it without an approved method and probability basis.

## Scenarios And Sensitivity

Scenarios are named input sets, not implied probabilities. Vary the few uncertain inputs that can change the decision, such as eligible volume, adoption, improvement, realization rate, and cost. Show the break-even input where possible; it often creates a better buyer question than a guessed central estimate.

## Validation And Approval

For each decision-driving unknown, identify the smallest measurement, customer confirmation, source document, or finance review needed. Keep authoring, customer validation, finance review, and approval as separate statuses. Pricing, contractual scope, delivery feasibility, accounting treatment, tax, legal claims, and public use require their authorized owners. Show customer, finance, commercial, delivery, legal, tax, and accounting review explicitly; use `Unknown` when applicability or ownership has not been established.
