# Evidence-Based Coaching Method

## Unit Of Analysis

Use the smallest supportable unit:

- **Episode:** one observable moment in one interaction;
- **Tentative pattern:** similar behavior appears more than once, but sample or context is limited;
- **Repeated pattern:** behavior recurs across a defined sample with counterexamples considered.

Never promote an episode into a repeated pattern because a manager uses the word “always.” Ask for the examples and locators.

## Observation To Experiment

1. **Observation:** What happened, where, and in what context?
2. **Interpretation:** What impact might it have had? Label alternatives.
3. **Target behavior:** What observable behavior should replace or extend it?
4. **Practice:** How will the rep rehearse safely?
5. **Live application:** Where can it be tried in real work?
6. **Signal:** What evidence would indicate progress?
7. **Failure signal:** What would show the hypothesis or intervention is wrong?
8. **Review:** What milestone or sample triggers reassessment?

## Evidence Quality

Consider source coverage, selection bias, call type, buyer behavior, deal complexity, territory, tenure, manager actions, enablement access, and system constraints. Outcome data can prioritize investigation but rarely identifies a coaching cause on its own.

Useful leading signals are tied to the behavior: for example, whether the rep tests a hypothesis with an open question and records the buyer's answer. Weak signals such as raw email count or talk ratio need context and should not become universal targets.

## Coaching Scope

Developmental coaching can organize evidence, practice, and learning. Formal ratings and employment actions require the organization's authorized process. Do not disguise a disciplinary decision as coaching or use an agent-generated plan as the sole basis for one.
