# Positioning And Messaging Methods

## Positioning Frame

A positioning choice links a defined audience and buying situation to a relevant alternative, differentiated capability, expected outcome, and supportable proof. It should make an important tradeoff; a statement that fits every audience and use case is usually too broad to guide action.

## Claim Ladder

Build each message from layers:

1. **Fact or capability:** directly supported product or market information.
2. **Interpretation:** why that fact may matter in the stated situation.
3. **Value hypothesis:** the operational or business outcome that could follow.
4. **Proof:** applicable customer, product, or measurement evidence.
5. **Dependency or limitation:** what must be true and where the claim does not apply.
6. **Validation:** the next test, source, or approval needed.

Never skip from capability to guaranteed outcome. Keep a strong product fact even when outcome proof is unknown; useful positioning can be honest about what remains to be validated.

## Differentiation

Compare on buyer-relevant dimensions and cite the source and date for external alternatives. Distinguish:

- meaningful strength from verified uniqueness;
- current capability from roadmap;
- direct competition from status quo, internal work, delay, and no decision;
- a supplier's description from observed buyer preference.

Do not repeat a competitor's marketing claim as objective truth or use private information without authorization.

## Message Architecture

A compact architecture contains:

- audience and buying situation;
- current-state tension in buyer language;
- desired outcome;
- positioning statement;
- two or three message pillars;
- proof and limitations for each pillar;
- likely objections and honest responses;
- language to use and avoid;
- a proportionate next decision or test.

Channel copy should inherit this architecture but adapt length, format, consent, and call to action for its context.

## Testing

Test one primary message variable while holding audience, channel, offer, delivery, and measurement window as stable as practical. Define a hypothesis, sample, exposure rules, success signal, failure signal, guardrail measure, and review dependency. Prefer qualified progression, comprehension, recall, and downstream outcome quality over attention metrics alone. A test can rank variants; it does not verify unsupported claims inside them.
