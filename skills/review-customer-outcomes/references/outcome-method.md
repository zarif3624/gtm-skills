# Customer Outcome Review Method

## Evidence Layers

Keep these layers separate:

1. **Activity:** events such as logins, workflows, messages, or support contacts.
2. **Adoption:** repeated use by the intended people for the intended workflow.
3. **Operational change:** a process, behavior, or decision changed.
4. **Business outcome:** a measurable result changed relative to a baseline.
5. **Realized value:** the customer validates that the outcome matters and is attributable enough for the current decision.

Evidence at one layer does not prove the next. High usage can coexist with workarounds, poor outcomes, or switching risk.

## Outcome Record

For each intended outcome, capture:

- authoritative source and scope;
- baseline and observation period;
- target or acceptance criterion and its approval state;
- current measure and source locator;
- attribution limits and counterevidence;
- customer validation status;
- obligation, dependency, and next validation step.

When no baseline exists, record current evidence as a starting observation, not improvement.

## Renewal Readiness

Review independently:

- value evidence;
- stakeholder alignment and change;
- product and support risk;
- delivery and unmet obligations;
- contract mechanics and commercial process;
- decision process and customer-confirmed timing;
- data coverage and contradictions.

Do not average these into a health color unless an approved model defines the calculation, evidence window, missing-data treatment, and decision use.

## Expansion Readiness

An expansion hypothesis needs evidence of realized or credible value, an additional customer problem, permission to explore, delivery capacity, and no unresolved issue that makes expansion premature. The smallest useful next step may be an outcome review, not a proposal.
