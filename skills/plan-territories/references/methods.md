# Territory Planning Methods

## Separate Three Quantities

- **Opportunity:** supportable potential for the seller's offer in the planning period.
- **Workload:** effort required to cover, research, engage, progress, and maintain the assigned accounts.
- **Capacity:** effort a role can responsibly supply after ramp, internal work, leave, and other commitments.

Do not use one proxy for all three. Employee count may relate to opportunity but say little about workload; account count says little about either.

## Workload Model

When measured time is unavailable, create provisional workload bands rather than precise hours:

| Work type | Example unit | Provisional weight |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Strategic account planning | Tier 1 account per period | 5 |
| Active complex opportunity | Opportunity per period | 4 |
| Multi-contact outbound | Account per cycle | 2 |
| Low-touch nurture | Account per cycle | 1 |

Calibrate weights with observed work and seller feedback. Do not treat the example weights as universal benchmarks.

## Capacity Model

Use:

`usable capacity = available work time × selling-work share × ramp factor`

Show the source and range for every input. Exclude time committed to management, enablement, leave, renewals, named projects, or other roles when appropriate. New-hire ramp and part-time availability are capacity inputs, not judgments of ability.

Represent each covered role with its own capacity factor `c_i`. Do not assign `c_i = 1` merely because no constraint was mentioned. Missing availability is `Unknown`, not full-time capacity.

## Scenario Tests

Compare scenarios on:

- opportunity and workload distribution;
- concentration in a few accounts or regions;
- coverage gaps and duplicate ownership;
- active-deal and customer continuity;
- required language, travel, licensing, or product expertise;
- partner and overlay interaction;
- sensitivity to missing or low-confidence fields.

Report median and range when useful, but inspect the extremes. Averages can hide one infeasible territory.

## Fairness And Governance

Define fairness operationally, such as comparable supportable opportunity, feasible workload, transparent rules, and equal access to exceptions. These can conflict. Record the tradeoff instead of claiming perfect fairness.

Keep a versioned assignment table with source fields, rule applied, exception reason, approver, effective date, and review date. Use a documented conflict path for parent-child accounts, inbound leads, active opportunities, relocations, and leave coverage.
