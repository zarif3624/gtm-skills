# Prepare Negotiation

Build a principled decision plan that protects buyer value, delivery feasibility, and authorized commercial boundaries. A good outcome can be an agreement, a revised path, a pause, or a clear no.

## Inputs

Read `.agents/gtm-context.md`, the deal plan, qualification evidence, proposal, pricing and packaging rules, approval matrix, contract or redlines, implementation scope, and prior buyer statements when available. Confirm:

- the buyer's desired outcome, unresolved risks, and stated commercial concerns;
- the seller's objectives, priorities, costs, delivery constraints, and alternatives;
- each issue in scope and the authoritative source for the current position;
- who can discuss, recommend, approve, sign, and deliver each term;
- the meeting objective, participants, sequence, and decision deadline—if verified.

Do not infer authority from title or meeting attendance. If policies or approval limits are missing, prepare questions and scenarios rather than usable commitments.

Read [the negotiation methods](references/methods.md) when designing packages, concessions, alternatives, approval paths, or meeting roles.

## Workflow

1. Separate verified buyer interests from positions and seller interpretations.
2. Create an issue map covering price, package, scope, volume, term, payment, services, implementation, support, risk allocation, publicity, and other relevant terms.
3. Record the current position, source, importance, flexibility, cost, dependency, and authority status for each issue.
4. Define the best supportable alternative, pause condition, and walk-away boundary. Label unapproved boundaries as proposals.
5. Generate two or three coherent packages that trade across issues instead of discounting one term in isolation.
6. Sequence conditional concessions from low cost to high cost. State every trade as a proposal, never an accepted agreement.
7. Prepare neutral questions that test the reason behind a request, who values it, and which alternatives could solve the same concern.
8. Map internal approvals and specialist review required before or after the meeting.
9. Assign meeting roles, note-taking, caucus signals, and a recap method.
10. Define what can be accepted live, what requires written approval, and what must be deferred.

## Guardrails

- Never invent approval, discount authority, margin, cost, buyer budget, competitor price, legal position, deadline, or executive support.
- Do not accept or draft final legal, security, privacy, tax, accounting, employment, insurance, or regulatory positions without the authorized specialist.
- Do not promise roadmap items, services, implementation dates, service levels, references, publicity, data rights, or custom work without approval from the owner who must deliver them.
- Do not use fake scarcity, expiring discounts, hidden terms, personal pressure, or threats.
- Do not disclose another customer's confidential pricing or terms.
- Keep `Discussable`, `Proposed`, `Approved`, `Accepted by buyer`, and `Executed` distinct. Silence or interest is not acceptance.
- Label suggested owners, approval steps, limits, dates, and packages as `Proposed` or `Unknown` unless a source confirms them.
- Do not optimize for signature at the cost of an unworkable or misleading agreement.

## Output

Use [the negotiation plan template](assets/negotiation-plan.md). Provide:

- objective, parties, source quality, and authority boundaries;
- buyer interests and seller priorities kept distinct;
- issue and approval map with current status;
- alternatives, pause conditions, and proposed walk-away boundaries;
- two or three conditional packages with tradeoffs and delivery impact;
- concession sequence and questions for each major request;
- meeting roles, caucus triggers, recap language, and next-step rules;
- claims and terms requiring commercial, finance, legal, security, product, or delivery review.

Do not produce a final agreement or claim that a proposal is approved. Preserve every unresolved approval and buyer decision in the recap.
