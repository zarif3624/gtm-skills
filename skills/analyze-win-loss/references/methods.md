# Win/Loss Analysis Methods

## Define Outcomes First

- **Won:** the buyer completed the defined purchase or expansion.
- **Lost:** the buyer selected a competing or alternative path.
- **No decision:** the evaluated change did not proceed in the analysis window.
- **Disqualified:** the opportunity did not meet explicit pursuit criteria.
- **Unknown:** the outcome or reason is not reliably established.

Keep no decision separate from competitive loss. Keep disqualification separate from seller-closed loss unless the team's reporting definition explicitly combines them.

## Source Strength

Prefer a triangulated decision record:

1. buyer interview or direct buyer statement;
2. traceable call, email, evaluation, or decision artifact;
3. contract, pricing, product, security, or process record;
4. seller or manager interpretation;
5. CRM reason code without supporting notes.

Source rank does not make a claim automatically true. Buyers, sellers, and records can each be incomplete. Preserve contradictions.

## Code Factors

Use a shared codebook and allow multiple contributing factors. Useful families include:

- problem priority and cost of no change;
- desired outcome and value evidence;
- product capability and implementation fit;
- trust, proof, security, and risk;
- stakeholder access and internal alignment;
- decision and commercial process;
- price, packaging, funding, and terms;
- competitor, internal alternative, delay, and no decision;
- seller execution and information quality.

Define every code in observable terms. Double-code a sample when multiple reviewers are involved and resolve meaningful disagreements.

## Compare Carefully

- Show the numerator, denominator, and missing count with every rate.
- Prefer like-for-like cohorts based on period, segment, product, deal size, source, or sales motion.
- Avoid slicing a small sample until a pattern appears.
- Look for counterexamples: wins with the alleged loss factor and losses without it.
- Treat interview participants as a potentially biased subset of the full population.

## Buyer Interviews

Ask for the decision sequence rather than validation of an internal theory:

- What changed, and what decision were you trying to make?
- Which alternatives did you seriously consider, including doing nothing?
- Which criteria mattered most, and how did they change?
- What increased or reduced confidence?
- Which event or unresolved issue most affected the final decision?
- What would have needed to be different for another outcome?

Do not argue, resell, request confidential competitor details, or promise a response. Record consent and approved quote use through the responsible process.
